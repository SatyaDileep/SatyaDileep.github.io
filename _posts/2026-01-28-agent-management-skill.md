One skill I'm really proud of - and believe everyone needs to develop - is something around **Agent Management**.

Your role will change with AI. In every domain, every practice, AI is drastically changing how we work. Enterprises are adopting these tools. But I'm not talking about that.

I'm talking about **Agent Management**.

## What It Means

You define certain agents that you control and use to achieve specific functionality. These agents can:

- Create images with specific color palettes
- Define user stories, tasks, and breakdown
- Draft articles, emails, and summaries
- Critique and refine outputs based on your feedback

Currently, I manage around 8-10 agents. Not that I use them every single day - but I know they're there when I need them.

## Why It Matters

I create these agents for repetitive tasks. Things that look boring. Things that take time but don't need my direct attention.

When I use them, I manage them - which means upgrading them as tools evolve. For example, when I started using Google Agent Space, it didn't have a search mechanism as a tool. As the market brought newer improvements (like Gemini's search integration), I upgraded my agents to leverage that capability.

## Sharing is Caring

These agents can't work alone. When you're facing issues or repetitive work, you need agents tailored to your context.

A person similar to you, working in the same role or the next level, will also need agents that help them. That's why sharing matters.

When someone adopts my agent and gives me feedback - "Can you add this additional functionality?" - it gives me a sense of pride. Someone else sees value in what I built.

## The Bigger Picture

At the end of the day:
- You're being productive
- You're making others productive
- You're learning how to apply AI tools
- You get continuous feedback to improve

This is what I think Agent Management is - and why I'm proud of building this skill.
