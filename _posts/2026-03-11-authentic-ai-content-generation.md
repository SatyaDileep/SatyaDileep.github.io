My fundamental principle for AI content generation is simple: the output must resemble you, not a different generic you. That's the secret to authentic content at scale.

## The Problem

AI content generators produce competent but generic output. It reads like it was written by everyone and no one. The tone is flat, the phrasing is predictable, and it lacks the quirks that make your writing recognizably yours.

## The Solution: Style Anchoring

The fix is straightforward. When you ask an AI to generate content, provide it with samples of your previous writing so it can calibrate to your actual voice.

Here's the workflow I use:

1. **Collect your writing samples**: Gather 3-5 of your best posts, articles, or emails. These should represent the tone you want to replicate.
2. **Feed them as context**: Include them in your prompt with a clear instruction - "Match this writing style."
3. **Specify what to emulate**: Call out the specific elements you want - sentence length, use of analogies, heading structure, level of formality.
4. **Review and adjust**: The first output will be close but not perfect. Give targeted feedback - "More direct openings," "Fewer adjectives," "Use numbered lists like I do."

## Why This Works

Large language models are excellent at pattern matching. When you give them enough of your writing, they can identify your structural preferences, your vocabulary range, and your rhetorical habits. The output shifts from "AI wrote this" to "this sounds like me."

## The Guardrail

AI should accelerate your voice, not replace it. Always review the output and make final edits. The goal is 80% there - you add the last 20% that only you can provide.

## The Bottom Line

Authentic AI-generated content is possible, but only if you anchor it to your actual writing. Don't let the model guess your voice. Show it.
