LLMs can learn from us, but there's a specific agentic design pattern humans desperately need to adopt: ReAct — Reason first, Act second.

## The Human Bug

In conversations — especially when receiving feedback or dealing with a blame game — our default setting is often to react instantly.

- Someone points a finger — we get defensive.
- Someone gives tough feedback — we start a debate.
- The result? Heated arguments, spoiled relationships, and a bad impression.

## The Agentic Fix

In the world of LLMs, the ReAct pattern is brilliant because it forces the system to reason first and act second. It creates a buffer where the model thinks through the context, identifies the goal, and then executes the step. It doesn't just spit out the first thought — it validates it against logic.

## My 2026 Implementation

I'm applying this to my own communication. Before responding to a trigger:

1. **Stop.** Create the latency.
2. **Reason.** Why are they saying this? What is my goal here? Is my immediate response helpful or just defensive?
3. **Act.** Respond based on logic, not just the impulse.

By giving ourselves that buffer for reasoning, we avoid unnecessary friction and maintain better professional relationships.

## Why This Matters

The same pattern that makes AI agents more reliable can make us more effective communicators. The difference between a reactive response and a reasoned one is the difference between escalating a conflict and resolving it.

Don't just react. ReAct.
