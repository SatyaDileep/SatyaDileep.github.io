For shipping new products, I follow a simple philosophy: MAYA — Most Advanced Yet Acceptable.

## The Principle

Think Tata Motors Sierra's comeback. Is it a relaunch or a smart strategy? It's both. Familiarity grabs attention — name recall, nostalgia, trust. Advanced features seal the deal — modern vibes, today's expectations.

The bottom line: when something is acceptable, it earns adoption. But to get that acceptance, a product must be familiar enough to feel comfortable and advanced enough to feel exciting.

## Why MAYA Works

People resist change that feels too foreign. They also resist products that feel outdated. The sweet spot is right in the middle — something that feels recognizable but pushes the boundary just enough to feel new.

- **Familiarity reduces friction**: Users don't need to relearn everything.
- **Advancement creates excitement**: Users feel they're getting something better, not just the same thing repackaged.
- **Together, they drive adoption**: Comfort gets people in the door. Innovation keeps them there.

## How to Apply MAYA

- **Keep the mental model**: Don't change how users think about the core task. Change how well it's executed.
- **Advance the experience**: Improve speed, accuracy, or delight — not the fundamental workflow.
- **Test the boundary**: Push as far as you can while keeping the product recognizable. If users say "this feels like X but better," you've nailed it.
- **Iterate toward advancement**: Start closer to familiar, then gradually introduce more advanced features as users adapt.

## The Bottom Line

Innovation for its own sake is a trap. The best products are the ones that feel familiar on day one and reveal their advancement over time. MAYA isn't about playing it safe — it's about making advancement acceptable.
