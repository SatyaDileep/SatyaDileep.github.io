### The Setup

I recently spent time diving into Google Opal, and the interface is undeniably slick. The combination of a minimalistic UI, real-time previews, and the ability to add diverse assets (drawings, text files, YouTube links) is fantastic for turning ideas into shareable micro-apps.

But the current reality check is important.

### Opal's Vibe Mode

I tried the "vibe mode," where you ask the AI to generate assets, and it's clearly in its very early experimental stages. Most of the time, it ignores the critical validation-first approach and misaligns expectations. Adjusting that output without manual intervention isn't easy.

### Canvas Comparison

When I put Opal's output side-by-side with my app built in Google Canvas, Opal's result looks half-baked. Canvas gives you more control over the iterative loop — you can refine, redirect, and validate at each step.

### The Simple Use Case

My fundamental principle is this: while using AI for content generation, the output must resemble **you**, not a different generic you. That's the secret to authentic creation.

Canvas respects this principle better today. It lets you provide previous content as a style reference, so the AI relates to your actual writing style and design preferences.

### The Bottom Line

Opal has tremendous potential, and the UI is a step forward. But for production-ready micro-apps that need validation, iteration, and personalization, Canvas is still the more reliable choice. I'm eagerly awaiting Opal's next set of iterative improvements.
