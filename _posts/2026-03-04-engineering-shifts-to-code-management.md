With AI agents advancing at this pace, I'm convinced that software engineering is shifting towards code management — design patterns, architecture, and orchestration.

## What's Changing

The act of writing code is becoming less of a differentiator. AI can generate functions, classes, and even entire modules in seconds. What it cannot do — yet — is make the architectural decisions that determine whether a system will scale, remain maintainable, and serve its users effectively.

## The New Engineering Skill Set

The engineers who will stand out are the ones who excel at:

- **Design patterns**: Knowing when to use a factory, when to use a strategy, when to compose versus inherit. AI can implement any pattern you tell it to — but it can't decide which one fits your problem.
- **System architecture**: Understanding how services communicate, where to put state, how to handle failure modes. These are judgment calls that require experience.
- **Code review and orchestration**: Reading AI-generated code, identifying flaws, and directing agents to iterate. The engineer becomes a conductor, not a solo performer.
- **Requirements translation**: Turning vague business needs into precise technical specifications that an agent can execute against.

## The Parallel With Other Industries

This shift mirrors what happened in manufacturing. The craftsman who hand-forged every part was replaced by the engineer who designed the assembly line. The value moved from execution to design.

Software is undergoing the same transition. The value is moving from writing code to designing systems.

## The Bottom Line

If your primary skill is "I can write code fast," AI is coming for that. If your primary skill is "I can design systems that work," AI is your multiplier.

Invest in architecture. Invest in patterns. Invest in the skills that let you manage code — not just write it.
